About this archive

Thailand UFO Files is a public, static dashboard of reported unidentified aerial phenomena, unusual lights and related sky events in Thailand. It is styled after a classified case-file cabinet from 1990s science-fiction television. The theme is for atmosphere only.

Disclaimer

Every entry is an unverified report aggregated from public sources. Nothing here is proof of extraterrestrial craft, secret programmes or supernatural beings. Many of the most dramatic lights have ordinary explanations: meteors and fireballs, the International Space Station, Starlink satellite trains, weather balloons, sky lanterns, clouds, squid-boat lamps, and rocket or satellite debris. Credibility tags are labels about the kind of source, not a score for “how alien” something looks. Coordinates are geocoded from named places and are often only as precise as a city, district or province.

What is in the data

The dataset aims at 2015–2026 and also keeps notable older public reports when a source supports them (for example 1968 filings from U-Tapao and Udorn). Quality was favoured over a round number. If a hundred firmly sourced, distinct Thai events could not be documented from reachable public pages, the archive does not invent the rest.

Sources

  • NUFORC (National UFO Reporting Center) public Thailand index. The live site presented a Cloudflare challenge from this build environment, so the index was read from a public Internet Archive snapshot dated 13 May 2026. Case links still point at nuforc.org. Witness text was paraphrased, not copied at length.
  • News and official science notes: Bangkok Post, The Nation, Thai PBS, Thairath, Manager Online, BBC Thai, The Thaiger, Thai Channel 8, PPTV, Matichon, and comments attributed to NARIT (the National Astronomical Research Institute of Thailand) or the Thai Astronomical Society.
  • Naga fireballs (บั้งไฟพญานาค): included only for years with a post-event count or a contemporary photograph — not from festival previews alone. Wikipedia’s overview is linked as background. The cause of the Mekong lights is disputed and is not settled here.

Sources that were skipped

nuforc.org could not be fetched live (Cloudflare). Reddit’s public JSON search returned an HTML interstitial, so no Reddit posts were added. MUFON was not available as an open public dump. Some news photo-galleries returned 404. Access walls and robots restrictions were not circumvented.

Photographs and illustrations

Every case file has a picture. That does not mean every case has a photograph of the event.

The only hosted photographs of a specific Thai event are from Wikimedia Commons: J A Forbes’s Beung Fai Phraya Nag series, taken on 19 October 2013 near Nong Khai (CC BY-SA 4.0). A few other Commons stills are used only as clearly captioned examples or location pictures: a Starlink satellite train (Jud McCranie, CC BY-SA 4.0), Yi Peng lanterns in Chiang Mai (Nik Cyclist, CC BY 2.0), the International Space Station after STS-134 undocking (NASA, public domain), a National Weather Service / NOAA weather balloon (public domain), fishing-boat lights in the Gulf of Thailand photographed from the ISS (NASA, public domain), meteor fireballs over ALMA and Kitt Peak (ESO/C. Malin and KPNO/NOIRLab/NSF/AURA/J. Dai, CC BY 4.0), and Mekong river views at Phon Phisai and east of Nong Khai (Jeremy Holton and per-schultz, CC BY 3.0). Example and location photographs are labelled as such and are not pictures of the Thai night in that case file.

All other cases use a generated X-Files-style illustration, stamped “ILLUSTRATION — NOT EVIDENCE,” with the case number and province overlaid. These are atmospheric art, never evidence. News-agency photographs are not copied or hosted; those cases keep a “View source” link. Credit and licence appear under every real photograph.

Map pins

Latitude and longitude are geocoded from the place name in the source (a beach, district, city or province). Precision is marked as area, province, or unspecified. A pin is never called “verified.” Reports with no usable place name appear in the case list only.

Sound

Browsers block audio before a user gesture, so the archive opens on an “ENTER THE FILES” splash. A click, tap or key starts a quiet, generated drone (sound on by default). A mute toggle in the header is saved in localStorage, and audio is silenced when the tab is hidden. The typewriter boot can be skipped and is skipped automatically if you prefer reduced motion.

How to host your own copy

This site is static files in the docs/ folder and is meant to be served by GitHub Pages from the main branch. To publish your own:

  1. Fork or clone github.com/piyaster11/thailand-ufo-files.
  2. In the new repository, open Settings → Pages and set the source to Deploy from a branch, branch main, folder /docs.
  3. After a few minutes the site will be at https://<your-username>.github.io/thailand-ufo-files/.

To rebuild the JSON after editing sources, run python3 pipeline/build_sightings.py from the repository root.
